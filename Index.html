Invisibles

A digital archive of women whose discoveries shaped science, but stayed out of the spotlight.

Behind almost every major scientific breakthrough, there are years of hard work that textbooks often leave out. Invisibles is a space dedicated to uncovering the forgotten stories of women researchers across all fields — from deep ocean mapping to the algorithms that sent humanity to the Moon.

The Archive

Oceanography

Marie Tharp

Created the first ocean floor map and proved continental drift theory.

Archaeology

Maria Reiche

Dedicated her life to researching and protecting the mysterious Nazca Lines in Peru.

Physics

Chien-Shiung Wu

The "First Lady of Physics" who disproved the law of conservation of parity.

Chemistry

Stephanie Kwolek

Invented Kevlar, a high-strength synthetic fiber saving millions of lives.

Astronomy

Henrietta Leavitt

Discovered the relation between luminosity and period of Cepheid variables.

Mathematics

Emmy Noether

Developed Noether's theorem, connecting symmetry and conservation laws in physics.

Botany / Ecology

Janaki Ammal

Pioneered sugarcane hybrid varieties and fought to preserve India's rainforests.

Medicine

Tu Youyou

Discovered Artemisinin, saving millions of lives from malaria.

Computer Science

Margaret Hamilton

Director of the Software Engineering Division that developed the Apollo guidance software.

Geology

Inge Lehmann

Discovered that Earth has a solid inner core inside a molten outer core.

Anthropology

Mary Leakey

Discovered the famous Laetoli hominin footprints preserved in volcanic ash.

Engineering

Hedy Lamarr

Co-invented frequency-hopping spread spectrum, the foundation for Wi-Fi & Bluetooth.
